# Hallucination Mitigation Techniques in Large Language Models: A Systematic Literature Review

This repository contains the research artifacts and literature collected for a **Systematic Literature Review (SLR) on hallucination mitigation techniques in Large Language Models (LLMs)**.

The review focuses specifically on approaches proposed to **reduce, prevent, correct, or control hallucinations** generated by LLMs. It brings together research from different perspectives and organizes existing mitigation techniques into a structured research landscape.

---

## About the Review

Large Language Models have achieved remarkable performance across a wide range of natural language processing tasks. However, their ability to generate fluent and convincing text does not guarantee that the generated information is factually correct or adequately supported by available evidence.

Hallucinations remain an important challenge affecting the reliability and trustworthiness of LLM-based systems.

Consequently, a wide range of mitigation techniques have been proposed in the literature, including approaches based on:

- Prompt engineering
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- External knowledge
- Fine-tuning
- Instruction tuning
- Reinforcement learning
- Decoding strategies
- Self-verification
- Self-correction
- External verification
- Tool augmentation
- Post-generation correction
- Hybrid approaches

This repository brings together and organizes the literature surrounding these approaches.

---

## Scope

The primary focus of this review is **hallucination mitigation in LLMs**.

The review considers research addressing questions such as:

- What techniques have been proposed to mitigate LLM hallucinations?
- How are existing mitigation techniques categorized?
- What mechanisms are used by different approaches?
- Which LLMs have been evaluated?
- What datasets and benchmarks are used?
- What evaluation metrics are reported?
- What limitations are identified?
- What research gaps remain?
- What emerging directions are being explored?

The repository is intended to provide a structured overview of the rapidly evolving research landscape.

## Research Gaps

An important goal of the review is to identify gaps in the current hallucination mitigation literature.

These may include gaps related to:

- Generalizability of mitigation techniques
- Evaluation consistency
- Benchmark limitations
- Computational requirements
- Model dependence
- Domain-specific hallucinations
- Multilingual LLMs
- Long-context generation
- Multimodal LLMs
- Reliability of automated evaluation
- Real-world deployment

The final research gaps will be derived from the systematic analysis of the included literature.
